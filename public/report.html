Caveats

Bandwidth & sizing

  • 30-second peak resolution. CloudWatch snaps period<60s up to 60s for NAT GW, but NAT GW publishes every 30s — Maximum at period=60 exposes the 30s bucket. Sub-30s microbursts can exceed reported peaks but won't sustain.
  • Per-VPC sizing. One c8gn size per VPC (homogenous across AZs) so EKS / workload rebalancing doesn't create asymmetric NAT instances. Cost = (hourly × 730) × AZs in that VPC.
  • "c8gn (cons)" vs "c8gn (recent)" columns. Two recommendations from two different sizing inputs:
    • cons — sized on the conservative input: max(90-day peak, AZ-aligned 1-min peak). Covers the worst spike we've seen in 90 days. This is what the Action column and Savings column are based on.
    • recent — sized on the last-15-day peak only. The "if current load is the new steady state, what's enough?" number.
    For most VPCs these match (shown as (same)). They diverge only on HISTORICAL-flagged VPCs. Use them as decision support, not as a recommendation pair — pick the column whose assumptions you believe.
  • HISTORICAL flag. When 90d peak / 15d peak ≥ 5×, the conservative sizing is anchored to an older event rather than current load. See the "What HISTORICAL means" note below.
  • Multi-flow IGW penalty. All NAT traffic is multi-flow through an IGW. <32 vCPU = hard 5 Gbps cap, ≥32 vCPU = ~50% of rated NIC. Reflected in the "Effective NAT Gbps" column.
  • PPS. Soft check assumes ~3 Mpps per 16 vCPU on Graviton4/Nitro v6 (no official number). Highest observed: ~2 Mpps. Not the binding constraint here.
  • Deleted NATs invisible (DescribeNatGateways state=available filter). me-central-1 region skipped (CloudWatch InternalFailure).

Cost

  • NAT cost from AWS Cost Explorer last 30d (USAGE_TYPE_GROUP filter on Running Hours + Data Processed). Per-VPC attribution: hours proportional to NAT count, bytes proportional to BytesInFromSource + BytesOutToSource last-30d totals per VPC.
  • EC2 alt cost = c8gn hourly (live AWS Pricing API) × 730h × AZs per VPC + 8 GB gp3 root volume per instance (baseline 3,000 IOPS / 125 MB/s included free). Two variants shown side-by-side:
    • OD — on-demand list price.
    • SP — 1-year Compute Savings Plan, No Upfront. Region-specific discounts pulled live from the savingsplans API: US regions ~28%, EU regions ~21-22%, Sydney ~27%, Tokyo ~17%. Note: Compute SP is the flexible plan (covers EC2/Fargate/Lambda); EC2-Instance SP would give deeper discounts (~40%) but locks to instance family/region — Laravel Cloud uses generic Compute SP today.
  • Internet egress excluded from both sides (unchanged $0.09/GB regardless of NAT path).
  • Still not modeled: NLB if used for HA, EIP hourly charges, intra-AZ data transfer, operational labor of self-managed NAT (AMI updates, on-call, failover testing). The bottom REPLACE accounts (savings under ~$200/mo) probably aren't worth the operational overhead even after these.
  • Action threshold: savings ≥ +$50/mo → REPLACE; ≤ −$50/mo → KEEP. Both OD and SP get their own Action verdict — SP can flip MARGINAL/KEEP cases to REPLACE since the EC2 cost drops ~20-28%.

What HISTORICAL means

The HISTORICAL flag is purely a math observation: the 90-day peak is at least 5× the last-15-day peak. Both numbers are accurate CloudWatch measurements — the flag does not mean the data is wrong or out of date. It means the conservative sizing recommendation is anchored to an event that hasn't happened in the last 15 days.

Three different operational stories produce the same flag:

  • One-off event. A migration, DR drill, customer onboarding bulk-load that's complete. Won't recur → safe to size for recent load.
  • Rare-but-recurring event. Monthly backup, quarterly bulk export, scheduled customer ETL. Will recur → size for the historical peak or accept reduced capacity at the next event.
  • Trend change. Traffic genuinely dropped (customer churned, workload migrated elsewhere). Won't return → size for recent load.

CloudWatch metric data alone can't distinguish these — you need product/operations context. The report shows both the conservative recommendation and the "recent" recommendation side-by-side so you can choose. The safe default is the conservative number, then re-evaluate after 30-60 days of EC2 NAT operation and downsize if recent peaks stay low.
